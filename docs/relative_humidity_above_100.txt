Humidity values above 100% are actually physically valid in meteorology. This is called supersaturation, and it can occur because of the following phenomena.
1. Sensor reporting relative humidity
Most modern sensors can report values slightly above 100% (typically up to ~103-105%) due to:
◦  Measurement uncertainty/calibration tolerances
◦  Transient supersaturation conditions during fog, dew, or precipitation events
◦  Sensor drift or condensation on the sensor itself
2. Real atmospheric supersaturation
Air can briefly hold more water vapor than its equilibrium saturation level, especially in:
◦  Fog formation conditions
◦  Cloud formation (though ground-level stations rarely see this)
◦  Rapid cooling events
